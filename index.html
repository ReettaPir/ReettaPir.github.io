What is tea?

Tea is a beverage made by soaking the leaves of the Camellia sinensis (or Aspalathus linearis in the case of Rooibos tea) plant in hot or cold water.

How is tea made?

Depending on the type of tea, the leaves are prepared either by drying, steaming, rolling, or oxidizing. These produce the signature color, aroma, and notes associated with each tea variant.

Chai or Tea?

The difference boils down to historical trading routes — the word "chai" was used in India and most of Asia, while the word "tea" was used in the West. In the West, the word "chai" often points specifically to chai masala — a type of tea mixed with milk and spices.

Tea Types

Explore the fragrant world of tea.

Black Tea

Distinguished by a strong flavor and reddish-brown color produced by the long oxidation process.

Green Tea

Made by quickly boiling the tea leaves after harvesting to prevent oxidation or withering. Distinguished by a mild earthy and grassy aroma as well as a greenish hue.

White Tea

Made by using young or minimally processed tea leaves to achieve a lighter flavor and aroma. Distinguished by a yellow hue.

Oolong Tea

Made by oxidizing the tea leaves under direct sunlight and allowing them to wither. Flavors can range from woody and thick to light and fresh aromas.

Rooibos Tea

Made by fermenting the tea leaves, which gives it a unique bright red color and a sweet nutty flavor. Rooibos tea is caffeine-free.

Herbal Tea

Made by using herbs, plants, or spices instead of the Camellia sinensis plant. The taste can vary from floral to spicy to earthy aromas.

Loose leaf or tea bag

Which one should you choose?

Loose-leaf tea is made using the whole leaf, which allows for more infusion and depth of flavor. You might also find more variety when it comes to selection. Using loose-leaf tea can be more eco-friendly compared to bagged tea.
Tea bags are made by using small particles, which makes it cheap to both produce and buy. They are also easy to use on the go. Compared to loose-leaf tea, the flavor is milder due to the leaves not having enough room to infuse with the water.
While longtime tea drinkers usually prefer loose-leaf tea, the preference is completely up to each individual. Personally, I enjoy tea bags due to their ease of use and preportioned sizing.
Tea is the second most consumed beverage in the world after water and contains less caffeine than coffee. Some variants also include antioxidants.
